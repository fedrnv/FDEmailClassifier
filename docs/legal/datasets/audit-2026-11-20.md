## License and provider interpretation

[MIT](https://opensource.org/license/mit) permits commercial reuse of material
covered by its grant, subject to notice retention. A metadata tag does not prove
ownership of all underlying content. For the GPT-generated candidate, current
[OpenAI Services Agreement](https://openai.com/policies/services-agreement/)
sections 3.3/4.1 and its Permitted Exception distinguish output ownership from
competitive-model restrictions; the classifier exception excludes distribution
or commercial availability to third parties. Those 2026 terms do not establish
which agreement governed the 2025 release, so the source remains unresolved.

[Apache-2.0](https://www.apache.org/licenses/LICENSE-2.0) permits commercial reuse
within its grant. Preserve the license, applicable attribution/NOTICE and change
notices when copies or derivatives require them; the card's optional request for
model attribution does not waive license conditions. [Anthropic commercial terms](https://www.anthropic.com/legal/commercial-terms)
sections B/D.4 separate output rights and competing-product restrictions. Its
[training clarification](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/12326764-can-i-use-my-outputs-to-train-an-ai-model)
specifically permits noncompeting specialist classifiers and excludes general
chat/open-ended generation. [OpenRouter terms](https://openrouter.ai/terms)
sections 5/6 defer output rights and restrictions to model-provider terms. This
audit therefore permits the narrow classifier purpose, not general model
distillation or an unrestricted generative training corpus.

The [original SpamAssassin corpus description](https://spamassassin.apache.org/old/publiccorpus/readme.html)
describes real messages, substantially intact headers and sender-owned copyright,
and warns against injecting them into a live mail system. The SpamAssassin
software project's license does not relicense those messages. Obtain an applicable
content grant and privacy basis before reconsidering this source.

## Operational boundary

`training/datasets/allowlist.json` binds the reviewed decision JSON, this report
and observations by canonical SHA256. Inclusion must specify exact dataset,
revision, artifact, purpose and `specialized-email-classifier` target. Changed
revisions, files or purposes require a new audit. The checked-in audit writer is
the trust boundary; hashes detect changed evidence, not legal truth or actor
authentication. A declared target is not proof of a training program's behavior.

The approved artifact's advertised SHA256 must be verified against downloaded
bytes before later ingestion; its payload was not downloaded in this task.
Opt-in loaders must retain source/revision/license records, avoid executing Hub
code and keep data outside Git. Synthetic provenance is the uploader's statement;
content/PII validation remains an ingestion obligation. Neither raw-data
redistribution nor trained-weight publication is approved here: they need their
own artifact/notice and model audits. Later training must retain the specialist
scope, and no multilingual accuracy follows from this English seed.

Blocked sources can be revisited with documented rights/provenance evidence and
a new reviewed audit binding. No upstream author was contacted or asked to
change licensing as part of this task.
